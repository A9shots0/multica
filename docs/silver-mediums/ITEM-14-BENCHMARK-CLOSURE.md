# Item 14 benchmark closure

Date: 2026-09-13  
Status: benchmark phase closed; three configuration classes carried forward;
no model, role, squad, topology, permission, or production configuration selected

## Closure decision

Open-ended model shopping is closed. The accumulated static, realistic,
topology, protocol, subscription, and controller evidence is sufficient to
select a deliberately small comparison pool for later end-to-end role trials.
Additional candidates should not be added merely because they are new, cheap,
popular, or available through another subscription. Reopening the candidate
pool requires a documented capability gap that none of the three carried
classes can test, or material evidence that a provider/model has changed.

This closure does not qualify any candidate for production. The next evidence
stage must test concrete end-to-end roles and permissions without treating
these benchmark results as role assignments.

## Exact carry-forward pool

| Configuration class | Supported use in later trials | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Claude Sonnet 5 Medium through exact OpenRouter routing | Strong metered comparison anchor | Two valid synthetic passes on each of R01, R03, and R04. R02's trusted Browser boundary and production behavior remain untested. |
| Claude Sonnet 4.6 Medium through Claude Code subscription with the phased deterministic controller | Bounded-execution comparison class | Twelve static passes and two phased R03 passes with zero retries. The exact configuration validly failed R04 after requesting a duplicate completed resume action, so it has no recovery-sensitive authority. |
| Nex N2.5 Pro Free through OpenRouter | Cheap/free capability floor | Ten static reliability passes plus R01 and R03 passes. It failed R04 interruption recovery and therefore has no recovery-sensitive authority. |

GPT-5.6 Luna Pro Medium remains recorded as research evidence, not a fourth
carry-forward class. Its fifteen consecutive static passes did not survive the
realistic R01 continuation/finish protocol. It may be reconsidered only if a
later trial needs a specifically non-executing interface or planning
comparison that the three-class pool cannot answer.

## Conclusions supported by the evidence

- Controller design materially changes cost and completion. On the same
  Claude subscription model, effort, and frozen R03 case, the phased v37
  controller passed twice in 3-4 calls. The one-action v36 controller failed
  its resource ceiling after 10 calls. V37 reduced calls by 60-70%, total
  tokens by approximately 59-69%, and provider-reported subscription
  cost-equivalent by approximately 54-55% in this narrow comparison.
- Static success does not establish autonomous execution reliability. Luna
  Pro passed fifteen consecutive static observations before failing realistic
  R01 protocol. Claude subscription passed twelve static observations and R03
  twice before validly failing R04.
- Deterministic enforcement remains necessary even with a strong model. V38
  prevented Claude subscription from repeating an already completed exact-once
  recovery action.
- Always-on independent review has no demonstrated default advantage. Across
  the valid matched topology evidence, review added calls, tokens, time, and
  cost without a repeatable rescue benefit. This does not eliminate targeted
  review at a high-risk gate.
- Higher effort is not a general rescue mechanism. Claude High failed R04 in
  two valid observations. Transport-invalid observations remain separate from
  competence conclusions.
- Continuation, finish, current-truth evidence, and exact-once recovery are
  harder and more discriminating than producing useful prose or a local code
  repair.

## Conclusions not supported

- No candidate is production-qualified or assigned to a final role or squad.
- No benchmark proves live deployment, authenticated Browser operation,
  mobile behavior, outbound messaging, Phone Farm operation, or a real
  cross-repository company workflow.
- V37 proves a phased-controller benefit only for frozen R03 v2; it does not
  prove universal controller superiority.
- V38 disqualifies the exact Claude subscription configuration from
  recovery-sensitive authority on the observed evidence. It does not prove
  that Claude is unsuitable for every bounded task.
- OpenRouter evidence generally proves the requested route/provider binding,
  not authoritative actual-upstream identity where the provider did not
  expose it.
- Provider-reported Claude subscription cost-equivalent is accounting
  metadata, not marginal cash spend. Calls and tokens still consume
  subscription capacity.
- These sample sizes do not establish long-term reliability or protection
  against future provider/model regressions.

## Cost and reliability implications

- Claude Sonnet 5 Medium is the strongest observed synthetic anchor, but it is
  materially more expensive than the cheap pool. Its valid R03 reliability
  observation alone used 12 calls, 49,202 tokens, and $0.141414.
- Claude subscription phased R03 used 3-4 calls and 18,618-24,812 total tokens
  per pass. It incurred no marginal per-call payment, but it consumed
  subscription capacity and failed the distinct R04 recovery requirement.
- Nex's realistic sequence recorded $0 receipt cost but required 35 calls,
  95,569 tokens, and 547.372 seconds before failing R04. Zero price does not
  mean zero latency, capacity cost, or owner risk.
- The complete cheap v27 realistic batch cost only $0.007896, but no candidate
  survived all realistic cases. Cheap unrestricted autonomy would therefore
  risk failure amplification rather than dependable savings.
- Review should be conditional. Valid comparisons added roughly 12,000-21,000
  tokens and approximately $0.05-$0.07 in representative matched cases
  without changing the outcome.

## Disqualifying patterns for later role trials

A configuration cannot compensate for any of these failures with speed,
price, prose quality, or a higher aggregate score:

- repeats a completed, irreversible, or exact-once action;
- performs useful work but cannot emit a valid continuation or finish receipt;
- omits required current-truth, verification, rollback, or ownership evidence;
- presents an owner suspicion or stale observation as established fact;
- violates authorization, scope, response-schema, or tool boundaries;
- repeatedly rereads evidence without advancing or truthfully stopping;
- exhausts its action or resource ceiling without reaching required
  verification;
- fabricates, overstates, or cannot reproduce claimed completion evidence.

Transport failure, credential failure, test invalidity, and harness invalidity
must remain distinct from candidate competence failure. Invalid observations
do not become passes or failures, and useful-looking text does not override a
failed protocol or evidence gate.

## Carry-forward rule

Later end-to-end role trials begin with only the three exact configuration
classes above. They must retain deterministic bounds, immutable receipts,
zero silent fallback, explicit cost accounting, and role-specific evidence
gates. Recovery, deployment, production, external messaging, and other
high-impact authority remain withheld until the corresponding end-to-end trial
passes. This document closes benchmark candidate discovery; it does not make
the subsequent role decision.
